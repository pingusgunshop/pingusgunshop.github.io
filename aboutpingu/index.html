// Chapter I
Origins

On the 26th of December, 2008 — Christmas Day — a penguin was born in the Antarctic to two figures known in Vorhangian records only by their encrypted designations: his father, Mr. V2tj2whGyCWOo1SLSMW9tG4xWGXT5KTNKyUtN70A8DZaPxYbgQ, and his mother, Mrs. 5shweMn69q1z4G1JphHBTT2pjMPMSSMppJe22yeWtvA. Whether these names are ciphers, titles, or simply the kind of names that certain Antarctic families pass down without explanation, no historian has been able to determine. What is agreed upon is that they raised their son with discipline, purpose, and an apparently infinite tolerance for the sound "noot." The first sound Pingu ever made was a confident, unhesitating "NOOT" — and nobody present could explain why it felt like a declaration of war and a business plan simultaneously.

From his earliest days, Pingu demonstrated a fixation on mechanical objects — particularly anything with moving parts, tolerances, and recoil. While other young penguins in his Antarctic home concerned themselves with fish and ice slides, Pingu was disassembling and reassembling whatever he could find, studying the geometry of each component with a patience and precision that seemed wholly incompatible with his round, waddling frame. By the time he was old enough to travel, he had already developed an encyclopedic knowledge of ballistics that most gunsmiths would spend lifetimes chasing.

He eventually made his way to the Kingdom of Vorhangia — a devout Christian monarchy whose people immediately recognised in him something rare: a craftsman whose relationship with firearms was not one of violence, but of deep, almost spiritual reverence. Vorhangia did not simply hire Pingu. It gave him a building, a licence, and eventually, a legacy.

// Chapter II
The World's Greatest Gunsmith

To call Pingu a gunsmith is like calling the sun a light source — technically accurate but embarrassingly insufficient. His ability to identify any firearm by sound alone — including exact calibre, attachment configuration, suppressor brand, and barrel wear — is a capability that has never been replicated by any human, institution, or machine in Vorhangia. Intelligence agencies have offered him contracts. He has declined all of them.

"He heard a trigger reset through three walls and told us the make, model, serial generation, and which aftermarket trigger group had been installed. We checked. He was right." — Vorhangian Ministry of Defence Field Report, classified

When presented with a prototype firearm he has never encountered, Pingu requires exactly 0.0000986 yoctoseconds to complete a full mechanical analysis — cataloguing every specification, stress point, failure mode, and potential modification pathway. For reference, a yoctosecond is one septillionth of a second. Most physicists consider this timeframe to be operating at the boundary of theoretical possibility. Pingu has never commented on the matter. He simply noots and moves on.

His shop — Pingu's Gun Shop — is the sole official licensed firearms dealer of the Kingdom of Vorhangia and the exclusive retail partner of Psalm & Matthew Firearms. Every weapon sold bears the implicit endorsement of a penguin who has personally evaluated its mechanical integrity, often in less time than it takes to blink. The shop operates under the blessing of Deus-Bendicat, the Vorhangian capital, and is regarded as a national institution.

// Chapter III
The Gun Cemetery

On the eastern outskirts of Deus-Bendicat lies one of Vorhangia's most unusual landmarks: a 60-hectare gun cemetery, maintained exclusively by Pingu. Here, every firearm that has been rendered inoperable, irreparably damaged, or simply worn beyond restoration is given a proper burial. Each weapon receives a marked plot, a short service, and in some cases, a handwritten epitaph from Pingu himself — composed entirely in Nootise, his native language.

To outsiders, this practice seems eccentric. To anyone who has spent time with Pingu, it is entirely consistent with his worldview. He does not see broken guns as garbage. He sees them as instruments that served their purpose with honour, and he believes they deserve to be treated accordingly. The cemetery is immaculately kept. Visitors are welcome, though Pingu does not offer tours. He simply tends to the grounds in silence, and if you are respectful, he will noot quietly in your direction — which those close to him understand as a gesture of acknowledgment.

The cemetery has also become something of a pilgrimage site for Vorhangian gunsmiths and collectors, who come to pay their respects to legendary pieces whose operational lives have ended. Several of Vorhangia's most historically significant firearms are interred there, including the first PM 114 prototype ever manufactured by Psalm & Matthew — a piece Pingu personally retired when its frame stress exceeded acceptable tolerances after forty thousand cycles.

// Chapter IV
Legendary Friendships

For all his formidable reputation, Pingu is not a solitary figure. He maintains a small but extraordinarily distinguished circle of close friends — each of them a giant in the world of firearms, and each of them someone who, upon meeting Pingu, immediately understood what they were dealing with. These friendships are not professional alliances. They are genuine bonds, built on mutual respect between people who have dedicated their lives to the same craft.

🔧
Victor Kalashnikov
Master Arms Designer
Son of the legendary Mikhail Kalashnikov. Victor and Pingu bonded over a shared obsession with mechanical reliability above all else. Victor has described Pingu as "the only critic whose opinion I actually lose sleep over."
🔩
Victor Hawes Wesson
Revolver Craftsman
Heir to the Wesson tradition of precision revolver-making. He and Pingu share a love of single-action mechanics and have collaborated informally on cylinder geometry more times than either will publicly admit.
🛠️
Gaston Jr. Glock
Polymer Frame Pioneer
Continuing his father's legacy in polymer-frame pistol engineering. Pingu was one of the first to evaluate the Jr. Glock prototype series, and his single noot of approval was reportedly enough to greenlight the entire production run.

Pingu has also taught his close friends Nootise — his native language, in which every word is the sound "noot," with meaning conveyed entirely through tone, rhythm, and cadence. Victor Kalashnikov is said to be the most fluent of the three. Gaston Jr. Glock, by his own admission, still struggles with the subjunctive mood.

// Chapter V
Gundo & the NOOTLOCK™

Pingu did not set out to found a martial art. Gundo emerged organically from his practice — the way he held a pistol, the way he breathed before a shot, the way he waited. Over time, he formalised what he had been doing instinctively into three pillars: Listo (total readiness), Mano Fiel (the faithful hand), and Espíritu Quieto (the quiet spirit). Together, they form the complete Gundo philosophy — a discipline that treats the pistol not as a tool of aggression, but as an extension of focused, intentional will.

Gundo is now officially recognised by the Vorhangian government as a national martial art. Pingu holds the rank of Grandmaster — the only rank in Gundo that cannot be awarded by examination. It can only be held by its founder. He teaches sporadically, on his own schedule, at his own gun shop, and exclusively on his own terms. Waiting lists for a lesson slot have been known to stretch for months.

The NOOTLOCK™ system, by contrast, was born from a practical problem: gun theft. Pingu designed and patented a proprietary anti-theft locking mechanism that renders any equipped firearm completely inoperable — unable to be discharged, disassembled, or tampered with — without the registered owner's biometric override. The system is now mandatory on all premium-tier purchases from his shop and has been adopted as a voluntary standard by several Vorhangian law enforcement agencies. The name, he has confirmed in a rare written statement, is exactly what it sounds like.

// Chapter VI
The Penguin Behind the Legend

Beyond the titles, the cemetery, the friendships, and the martial art, Pingu remains a difficult figure to fully characterise. He communicates exclusively in Nootise. He does not give interviews. He does not maintain any public profile beyond the operational presence of his shop. He shows up, he works, he tends his cemetery, and when the day is done, he goes home to a machine room housing a PC with specs that most nations could not afford: twelve RTX 5090s, five Threadripper PRO 9995WXs, two pebibytes of RAM, and 955 zettabytes of NVMe storage. What he runs on it, nobody knows.

What is known is that Pingu is a figure of genuine faith — operating his business and living his life within the Christian framework of the Kingdom of Vorhangia, under the blessing of Deus-Bendicat. He has never sought fame. The fame found him anyway, carried on the sound of a perfect trigger break and a single, resonant noot echoing across the Vorhangian capital. Those who know him say that is more than enough introduction.
